## 2. Price Range
- **Lowest:** Free (Ollama open-source running on own hardware).
- **Median:** €290/month (Compliance tier / standard SaaS cloud subscriptions) up to pay-per-token/hour.
- **Highest:** Custom enterprise contracts (€10k+/month for T-Systems / Aleph Alpha / STACKIT sovereign clusters).

## 3. Positioning Map
- **Axes:** Compliance/Sovereignty (Low → High) vs. Specialization for Regulated Secret Holders / § 203 StGB (Generic Cloud/API → Personal Legal Verpflichtung & Audit Trail).
- *IONOS / STACKIT / T-Systems:* High sovereignty & C5 certification, but generic cloud providers whose legal counsel will *not* sign personal § 203 StGB criminal liability addendums.
- *Beck-Noxtua:* High legal vertical integration, but closed ecosystem (Beck library) rather than flexible developer workflows or custom inference endpoints.
- *konforme-ki.de:* Direct focus on Berufsgeheimnisträger (§ 203 StGB) with personal executive sign-off on server operation and cryptographic hash-chained audit trails.

## 4. Market Friction & Complaints (Public Sources / Synthesis)
- **Enterprise Red Tape & Lock-in:** Large sovereign clouds require lengthy procurement, BSI C5 audit reviews, and complex DORA compliance registers.
- **Lack of Personal Secrecy Commitment:** Large cloud compliance departments refuse personal criminal liability agreements under § 203 StGB.
- **Opacity of AI Outputs:** Enterprises and law firms cannot cryptographically prove to auditors or chambers exactly which model/hardware processed a query and whether data was retained.

## 5. The Gap
konforme-ki.de owns the specific wedge where **large cloud providers refuse to go**: signing a personal § 203 StGB Verschwiegenheitsvereinbarung (backed by a German GmbH operating its own hardware in Nürnberg) coupled with an immutable, hash-chained audit trail per request. While IONOS and STACKIT win generic IT procurement, they lose on personal professional secrecy compliance for small-to-medium law practices and medical professionals.

## 6. The Threat
Large vertical legal tech platforms (like Wolters Kluwer / Beck) or specialized German legal-AI wrappers could build dedicated secret-holder compliance wrappers around sovereign backends. However, their reliance on third-party hyperscalers (AWS/Azure) still exposes them to foreign jurisdiction risks unless they operate bare-metal German hardware with executive sign-off.
